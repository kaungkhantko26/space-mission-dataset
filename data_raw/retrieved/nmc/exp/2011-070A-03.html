Mars Descent Imager (MARDI)

NSSDCA ID: 2011-070A-03

Mission Name: Mars Science Laboratory (MSL)
Principal Investigator:Dr. Michael C. Malin

Description

The MARs Descent Imager (MARDI) is designed to take pictures of the martian surface below the rover as it descends for landing. The primary objectives of MARDI are to determine the exact location of the landing and provide a geologic and engineering-geologic framework of the landing site for early operations. MARDI MARDI is mounted to the fore-port side of the MSL rover, even with the bottom of the rover chassis. It points towards the ground in the rover coordinate frame and therefore downward during the descent phase. Imaging starts after heat shield separation and continues until touchdown. The camera takes approximately 5 frames/second, written into a flash memory for later transmission to Earth. The field of view is 70 x 55 degrees (1600 x 1200 pixels), ranging from 2.4 x 1.8 km (1.5 m/pixel) at 2 km altitude, to 2.4 x 1.8 m (1.5 mm/pixel) at 2 m altitude. Instantaneous field of view (IFOV) is approximately 0.76 milliradians.
