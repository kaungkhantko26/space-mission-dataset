Radiation Assessment Detector (RAD)

NSSDCA ID: 2011-070A-10

Mission Name: Mars Science Laboratory (MSL)
Principal Investigator:Dr. Donald M. Hassler

Description

The Radiation Assessment Detector (RAD) is designed to characterize the full spectrum of energetic particle radiation at the surface of mars, including galactic cosmic rays, solar energetic particles, secondary neutrons, and other particles created both in the atmosphere and in the martian regolith. Other primary science objectives are to determine the radiation dose rate for humans on Mars; enable validation of Mars atmospheric transport codes; study transport of heavier-than-helium particles through the martian atmosphere; characterize the radiation hazard and mutagenic influences for past and present life on Mars, at and below the surface; and constrain chemical and isotopic effects of radiation on the martian surface and atmosphere.

RAD is an energetic particle detector mounted just below the top deck of the rover. It consists of a charged particle telescope pointed in the zenith direction comprising three solid state detectors and a cesium iodide (CsI) calorimeter. A BC-432 scintillating plastic channel is used with the CsI calorimeter and an anti-coincidence shield to detect and characterize neutral particles.
