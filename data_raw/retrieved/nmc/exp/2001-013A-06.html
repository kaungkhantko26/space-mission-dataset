Radio Science

NSSDCA ID: 2001-013A-06

Mission Name: 2001 Mars Odyssey
Principal Investigator:Prof. G. Leonard Tyler

Description

There were no recognized radio science investigations planned on the 2001 Mars Odyssey mission but the radio tracking data collected as part of the normal radio communications and navigation functions of spacecraft operations could be used to improve knowledge of the Mars gravity field and might have other applications, such as radio occultation studies of the atmosphere.

The spacecraft radio system was constructed around a redundant pair of X-band Small Deep Space Transponders (SDSTs). Data was received at 7155.377315 MHz and transmitted at 8406.851852 MHz via one of three antennas. A 1.3 meter high-gain steerable dish antenna could transmit and receive, a medium-gain antenna was used for transmission only, and an omni-directional low-gain antenna used for reception only. Also integrated in the communications system were two redundant solid state power amplifiers (SSPAs) and a diplexer. The SDSTs were connected to redundant Command and Data Handling (C&DH) units in such a way that any pairing could be chosen. SSPA output power design was for 15 W (41.8 dBm) at end of life. The radio comunications take place with Deep Space Network (DSN) stations on Earth.

The ODY telecommunications system was designed to perform the following functions: 1) Receive an X-band uplink carrier from a DSN station and demodulate the command data and ranging signal if either were present; 2) Generate an X-band downlink carrier either by coherently multiplying the frequency of the uplink carrier by the turn-around ratio of 880/749 or by utilizing an auxiliary crystal oscillator (AUX OSC); 3) Phase modulate the downlink carrier with either (or both) of the following: a composite telemetry signal, consisting of a square wave subcarrier (25 kHz or 375 kHz) that was BPSK (binary phase shift keying) modulated by telemetry data provided by the C&DH subsystem; the ranging signal that was demodulated from the uplink; 4) Permit control of the telecom subsystem through commands to select signal routing and the operational mode of the subsystem either from the ground or from command sequences previously loaded on the spacecraft; 5) Provide telecom status for monitoring operating conditions of the subsystem; 6) Provide ON/OFF power control for all RF transmitters; 7) Assume a single well-defined operating mode (a known baseline state) after a Power-On-Reset (POR).

The data are in the form of ASCII files. Each file has a detached label file which describes the contents and format of the data file. Documentation on the files and data is included on each CD.
