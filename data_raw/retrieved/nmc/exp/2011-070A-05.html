Sample Analysis at Mars (SAM)

NSSDCA ID: 2011-070A-05

Mission Name: Mars Science Laboratory (MSL)
Principal Investigator:Dr. Paul R. Mahaffy

Description

The primary scientific objective of the Sample Analysis at Mars (SAM) instrument suite is to study the present and past habitability of Mars by exploring molecular and elemental chemistry relevant to life. SAM addresses carbon chemistry through a search for organic compounds, chemical state of light elements other than carbon, and isotopic tracers of planetary change. The measurement goals are to: survey carbon compound sources and evaluate their possible mechanisms of formation and destruction; search for organic compounds of biotic and prebiotic importance, including methane; reveal the chemical and isotopic state of elements that are important to life as we know it; determine the atmospheric composition including trace species that are evidence of interactions between the atmosphere and soil; and better constrain models of atmospheric and climatic evolution through measurements of noble gas and light element isotopes.

SAM comprises three instruments: a Quadropole Mass Spectrometer (QMS); a Gas Chromatograph (GC); and a Tunable Laser Spectrometer (TLS). It also includes a Sample Manipulation System (SMS) and a Chemical Sparation and Processing Laboratory (CSPL) with a series of valves, gas manifolds with heaters and temperature monitors, chemical and mechanical pumps, carrier gas reservoirs and regulators, pressure monitors, pyrolysis ovens, and chemical scrubbers and getters for atmospheric gas measurements. CSPL valves and pumps are used to bring gas through an inlet tube to the SAM instruments. For solid surface material, the sample is sieved and deposited in one of the 74 SMS sample cups. The cup is placed in the SAM oven and heated to allow release of volatiles, which can be channeled to the appropriate instrument. The QMS and GC are used for identification of organic compounds, the TLS can measure isotope ratios for C and O in carbon dioxide and trace levels of methane and its carbon isotopes.
