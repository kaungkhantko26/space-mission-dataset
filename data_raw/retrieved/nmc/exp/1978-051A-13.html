Electric Field Detector (OEFD)

NSSDCA ID: 1978-051A-13

Mission Name: Pioneer Venus Orbiter
Principal Investigator:Dr. Robert J. Strangeway

Description

The Electric Field Detector (OEFD) experiment consisted of a modified version of the Pioneer 8 and Pioneer 9 experiments to measure the electric-field components in the frequency region from roughly 50 to 50,000 Hz. The aims of the investigation were to perform an analysis of VLF electric fields at Venus and to elucidate the plasma interactions between the solar wind and the ionospheric or exospheric plasma and how the solar wind is deflected around Venus. The role of plasma instabilities in modifying the heat flux from the solar wind and in thermalizing newly-born ions from Venus, the variable location of the Venus bow shock, ionopause, and wake cavity boundary, the extent of the upstream turbulence region, and the effects of wave-particle interactions within the Venus ionosphere were also studied. Secondary objectives were a search for whistler mode electromagnetic noise bursts (around 100 Hz) and analysis of solar wind disturbances at the Venus orbit and in cruise.

A self-contained balanced V-type electric dipole antenna with a differential preamplifier was employed to make the measurements. The electronics box had two levels, the upper box was 12.2 x 6.6 x 5.5 cm, and the lower 19 x 6.6 x 2 cm, with a total mass of 5 kg. The electronics contained RF filters, a preamplifier, four channel filters and amplifiers, a power converter, and control logic. The 0.5 kg antenna unit was mounted directly on the box. The two antenna elements were 69 cm long with a 10.5 cm diameter spherical wire grid at the end, giving an effective antenna length of 75 cm, and a sphere-to-sphere separation of 76 cm. They were deployed in the spacecraft equatorial plane, perpendicular to the spin axis, in a vee shape at an approximately 60 degree angle (each ~30 degrees from a radial line). The instrument had a total mass of 0.55 kg and used 0.5 W power.

Measurements were made in four ~30%, narrow-band channels centered at 100, 730, 5,400, and 30,000 Hz. Each channel had particular usefulness at various points along the orbit, depending on the density of the solar wind and the characteristic plasma wave frequencies. The instrument measured the electric field plasma wave frequency in each channel, amplified it, converted the four analog outputs to digital form, and transmitted them to Earth. A minor frame contained 512 bits, transmission rates ranged from 4 minor frames per second to 1 minor frame every 64 seconds. Typical rates near periapsis were 2 t0 4 minor frames per second. In most telemetry modes a minor frame contained a readout from all 4 channels.
