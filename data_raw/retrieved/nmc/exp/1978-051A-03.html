Gas-Plasma Environment-Dual Frequency Experiment (OGPE)

NSSDCA ID: 1978-051A-03

Mission Name: Pioneer Venus Orbiter
Principal Investigator:Dr. Thomas A. Croft

Description

The Gas-Plasma Environment-Dual Frequency Experiment (OGPE) used data obtained from the S-band and X-band radio signals. The objectives were (1) to determine the lateral variations in the Venusian atmosphere and ionosphere, (2) to study the solar wind microscopic flow, and (3) to analyze solar wind scintillations. The experiment used the existing Pioneer Venus radio system to study variations in radio signal strength and phase, or scinitillations, caused by turbulence in the solar wind, and changes in radio dignals caused by their passage through the Venus atmosphere (see the Radio Occultation Experiment record for more information on the atmospheric technique).

The solar wind and solar corona studies were done when Venus was close to superior conjunction with the Sun. The radio waves from the spacecraft then passed close to the Sun on their way to Earth, passing through the densest region of solar wind. Two Deep Space Network stations were used simultaneously to record the fluctuations in the S-band and X-band signals. Phase scintillations and spectral-broadening measurements can be measured, giving data on the refractive index of the medium, which is a function of the distribution of free electrons. This can provide information about solar wind density, turbulence, and velocity.

Pioneer Venus Orbiter Radio System

1.09 m diameter high-gain parabolic dish antenna mounted on a 2.9 meter mast on the top deck of the spacecraft. It is despun from the rotating spacecraft so that it always faces Earth. It is also capable of being pointed up to 15 degrees from the Earth during Venus atmospheric occultations, to account for the refraction of the beam as it passed through the atmosphere. It operated in both S and X bands. At S-band, the antenna beam is 7.6 degrees. The orbiter carries a 750 mW X band (8.415 GHz) transmitter and a 2.295 GHz S band transmitter. The frequencies are set so that the X-band frequency is 11/3 of the S band.
