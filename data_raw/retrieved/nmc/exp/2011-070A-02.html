Mars Hand Lens Imager (MAHLI)

NSSDCA ID: 2011-070A-02

Mission Name: Mars Science Laboratory (MSL)
Principal Investigator:Mr. Kenneth S. Edgett

Description

The primary objective of the MArs Hand Lens Imager (MAHLI) is to acquire images at high magnification (hand lens scale) of chosen rock and regolith targets. The high magnification enables study of the petrography and mineralogy of the rocks. MAHLI images are also used for identification of samples for other instruments, such as APXS, CheMin, or SAM.

MAHLI is mounted on the turret at the end of the robotic arm. The imager can focus at distances between 20.4 mm and infinity, with a resolution as high as 13.9 microns/pixel. At 25 mm the field of view is 34 degrees diagonal with an effective focal length of 18.3 mm at f/9.8. At infinity, the field of view is 39.4 degrees diagonal with effective focal length of 21.3 mm at f/8.5. The instrument bandpass is 380 to 680 nm.

For more detailed information, see:

http://msl-scicorner.jpl.nasa.gov/Instruments/MAHLI/
