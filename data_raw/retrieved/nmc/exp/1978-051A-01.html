Electron Temperature Probe (OETP)

NSSDCA ID: 1978-051A-01

Mission Name: Pioneer Venus Orbiter

Description

The Orbiter Electron Temperature Probe (OETP) consisted of a pair of cylindrical Langmuir probes of the type used on the Atmospheric Explorer (AE) series. Two probes were required, so that one was always out of the wake of the spacecraft. The results of these high-resolution measurements were used both to study the upper atmosphere and the ionosphere and to investigate the interaction of the solar wind with the Venusian ionosphere.

The OETP consists of two cylindrical Langmuir probes, called the radial sensor and axial sensor, mounted on booms, with a central electronics unit inside the orbiter bus. The radial sensor is mounted on the end of a 1 meter boom that extends from the rim of the cylindrical spacecraft perpendicular to the spin axis. The axial sensor is on the end of a 0.4 meter boom extending from the forward deck, slightly in from the edge and parallel to the spin axis. Each probe collector is attached to an exposed 2.5 cm long teflon guard electrode at the end of the boom.

The teflon guard is the exposed end of the inner rod of the rigid triaxial boom, it is held at the probe voltage. The rod is surrounded by the outer boom, composed of titanium and teflon, and is held at the spacecraft ground potential. The probes and boom are connected to electrometers to monitor the voltages of each. The probe collector is 5 cm long and 0.42 cm in diameter. A surface layer of rhenium is deposited on the radial probe, and molybdenum on the axial probe. The central electronics contains electrometer amplifiers and adaptive sweep voltage generators, as well as an A/D converter, data handling circuitry, signal multiplexing, and first in / first out memory.

Sweep voltages are applied to the probes in 0.5 second cycles. In each cycle, the ion density can be determined from the electrometer output just prior to the sweep, the electron temperature from the change in probe voltage that produces a change equal to a factor of e, and the electron density from the electrometer output when the sensor is driven in to the electron saturation region.

The OETP would operate when the spacecraft orbit brought it within the ionosphere of Venus. Several hundred volt-amp curves would be taken within a single pass, but due to the limited telemetry rate available to each experiment, inflight processing was done prior to transmission. Measurements were made at 1132 b/s, the optimum output data rate for the instrument was 80, 128, or 160 b/s. Various data formats could be selected by command. In flight, 56 measurements taken at a rate of one per second provided high spatial resolution for the measurements of electron density and temperature. This experiment provided measurements over the whole region traversed by the orbiter, covering a large range of solar aspect angles, to yield a more complete configuration of the physical properties of the ionopause region.
